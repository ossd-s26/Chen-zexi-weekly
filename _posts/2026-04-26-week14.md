## Update on the OpenTelemetry PR

Last week I wrote about [PR #3173](https://github.com/HKUDS/nanobot/pull/3173), the OpenTelemetry tracing contribution to Nanobot. A week in, the PR has gotten real engagement from the maintainer but it's not headed for a merge before the semester ends, and that's been an interesting outcome to sit with.

The maintainer's main feedback was a design-level pushback. They prefer a "completely non-intrusive" observation approach — similar to how Nanobot already wires Langfuse in via the SDK directly — over the OpenTelemetry middleware approach my PR takes.

The deeper question is *how far into the codebase observability should reach*. There's a spectrum:

- **Shallow** — hook into LLM provider calls only. This is roughly what the existing Langfuse integration does. Minimal code, easy to maintain, but you only see individual API requests, not the agent's reasoning trajectory.
- **Medium** — wrap at the provider level so retries and full request/response shape come for free, but the agent loop itself stays opaque from the outside.
- **Deep** — instrument the full agent loop end-to-end: a root span for message processing, child spans for each LLM call (including retries), and tool execution spans nested underneath. This is what my PR does. You get the trajectory view, but the instrumentation has to touch `loop.py`, `runner.py`, and `providers/base.py` — a real maintenance surface for the project to carry going forward.

The tradeoff isn't just vendor-neutral vs vendor-rich; it's how much of the core codebase you're willing to let the observability layer touch. A maintainer optimizing for keeping the core loop legible will pick shallow almost every time. A user trying to debug an agent that went sideways will want deep. Both are reasonable, they just optimize for different people, and the right answer depends on who the project sees as its primary audience right now. That's a call only the maintainer can make, which is part of why I'm comfortable letting this one sit.

## Working as a Group on an Outside Project

Our team has been meeting weekly to share progress and plan next steps. Early on, the plan was for the four of us to take on a single big feature together, but we couldn't find a candidate in Nanobot's issue tracker that was scoped right for that — the features big enough to be "group-sized" were either already being worked on or would have needed more upfront architectural agreement with the maintainers than we'd built up. So we pivoted: each of us picked our own issues and PRs to work on individually instead.

That shaped what the weekly meetings became. They settled into two main things: (1) sharing progress and giving feedback on each other's work, and (2) helping each other come up with ideas for what to tackle next.

With our presentation tomorrow, the project is wrapping up. Looking back, the group functioning as a feedback and ideation circle rather than a single coding unit was probably the right shape for a project of this size and timeline. Everyone got their own contribution narrative, and we still got the benefits of working alongside people who understood what each other was doing.
